Company focus

Manufacturer of collaborative arms, quadrupeds, industrial AMRs and mobile dual-arm research platforms with distinct historical KAIST lineage

  • The current RBC family lists ten named six-axis arms with metric payloads from 3 to 30 kg and tabulated reach from 730 to 1900 mm. Exact mounting footprint and robot mass are separate from controller dimensions and weight. In-house reducers, brakes and control technology are manufacturer-positioning claims, not a quantified market-leadership result.
  • The three named RBC-N arms target food-and-beverage processes with SUS304-based hygienic housing, IP66 and manufacturer NSF/ANSI 169 statements. World-first, safety and certificate claims have not been independently checked; an N name is not interchangeable with a standard arm or a complete cooking cell.
  • Exact-model protection differs: RB3-730 and RB5-850 tables list IP54; other accessed standard-arm tables list IP66. No model-specific protection rating is assigned to RB16-900 from neighbours. The family-card product query returns the homepage despite HTTP 200, so its missing detail specifications remain unverified.
  • RB5-850 and RB5-850N narrative reach is 850 mm versus 927.7 mm in their tables/cards. RB5-850 pairs 5 kg with 6.6 lb; RB30-1400 pairs 71 kg with 158 lb. These metric/imperial and narrative/table conflicts stay visible, without choosing a corrected number or inventing a version mapping.
  • The RBQ-10 page gives conflicting walking speeds (6 versus 9 km/h) and step heights (20 versus 25 cm). Running up to 14 km/h and longitudinal/lateral slopes of 45%/20% retain their mode and percentage units. Two-hour/up-to-four-hour operation has no reviewed workload/configuration conditions.
  • RBQ-10 exposes LV0/LV1 control and ROS 2/DDS development interfaces; LiDAR, LTE, surveillance modules and wireless charging depend on configuration. Automatic recharge support is separate from charge duration and single-charge endurance.
  • The expanded portfolio includes RBM-D400/D1000 and RBM-S100a/S100b AMRs. Exact-model payloads are 400/1,000/120/100 kg and masses 70/150/approximately 50/70 kg; S100 family naming does not imply a uniform 100 kg payload. Sensor-detection height, cargo boxes and optional chargers are separate from robot terrain, dimensions and endurance.
  • RB-Y1 is a configurable wheeled dual-arm research platform with SDK/ROS 2 and teleoperation interfaces. Official DOF tables disagree within and between sources. Current 1.5 m/s and historical 2024 introduction 2.5 m/s remain qualified. YPY/YPR wrists, A/M bases, neck/gripper/vision and leader-arm devices retain configuration scope rather than generating extra whole robots.
  • RB-Y2 and RBQ-10W are separately named coming-soon platforms. Product placeholders and simulator wheel models do not establish delivered specifications or shipment. Generic Mobile Manipulator, ASTRO integrations, FMS, chargers and expansion modules remain system/software/accessory findings.
  • Original HUBO, HUBO 2, DRC-HUBO, FX-2 and RBQ-3 retain distinct university/manufacturer identities and dated demonstration scope. FX-2 was human-ridden; a HUBO 2 news-anchor appearance does not establish autonomous journalism. The current RBQ manual labels RBQ10 and is not RBQ-3 technical evidence.
  • The manufacturer heritage describes a 2004 HUBO development milestone, while KAIST identifies Rainbow Robotics as established in 2011. Neither university-era heritage nor the later DARPA competition makes all historical research robots current retail products. Full historical/regional coverage, independent task outcomes and achieved market leadership remain unproven.
